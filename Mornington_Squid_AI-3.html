Current conditions
Water clarity (most important) — Southern calamari are visual hunters. Clarity is estimated from rain, onshore wind, waves/swell and current. Dirty water is the #1 session killer in Port Phillip Bay.
Light windows — First light (dawn + ~90 min) is the strongest single window. Dusk and early night under pier lights are also heavily favoured. Deep night is useful but slightly less than the early pier-light period. Midday is penalised in shallow water.
Tide behaviour — Explicit preference for early flood (rising tide while still near high) plus slack-to-high. Strong runs hurt jig presentation around structure.
Classic alignment bonus — The model specifically rewards light-change periods that line up with good tide movement (the sessions locals talk about). Rising tide into dusk/dawn in clear water receives the strongest lift.
Temperature vs clarity — Lab work shows higher attack rates when warmer, but real PPB results are driven more by clear cooler water. The model gives a mild predatory-drive lift around mid-teens SST while still letting clarity dominate.
Night under lights vs pure darkness — Early night (roughly 19:30–22:30) is treated as a pier-light window and scores higher than the middle of the night. Darker moons help concentrate activity under the lights.
Wind & fishability — Light wind is best for line control and feel. Onshore wind both dirties water and makes the pier less pleasant. Strong wind + poor clarity triggers hard “stay home” gates.
Honest limits — Transparent behavioural heuristic grounded in local knowledge and published southern calamari research. Not a calibrated scientific forecast. Marine sea level is a model proxy. Always check actual water colour on arrival.
